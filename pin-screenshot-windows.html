Windows screenshot workflow

How to pin a screenshot on top of other windows

Keep a visual reference visible while you code, write, design, or follow instructions.

Why keep a screenshot visible?

Sometimes you need to look at a diagram, error message, design reference, or set of instructions while using another application. Switching back and forth between windows breaks your focus and makes simple tasks feel slower.

A useful screenshot workflow is: capture the important area, copy or save it, then keep that image visible above your other windows until you no longer need it.

A simple three-step workflow

1. Capture a region

Use a keyboard shortcut to bring up the capture tool, then drag around the part of the screen that matters. A region capture is usually faster and clearer than saving the entire desktop.

2. Pin the capture

Pin the screenshot to the desktop so it stays visible while you work in another window. You can move it out of the way, resize it, or close it when you are finished.

3. Keep working

Paste the capture into a message or document, use the saved file later, or leave it floating as a temporary reference. The screenshot should support your work without becoming another full application to manage.
